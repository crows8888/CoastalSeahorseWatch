Vision, Mission & Coastal Scope

The Coastal Seahorse Watch Initiative

Vision Statement: We envision thriving, protected ocean ecosystems where resilient seahorse populations serve as visible indicators of healthy marine life.

Mission Statement: To engage divers, coastal residents, and students in gathering critical ecological data on seahorses to drive community-led habitat protection and marine conservation policy.

Core Monitoring Focus: Monitoring shallow-water seahorse populations, tracking seagrass habitat loss, and reducing accidental bycatch from local fisheries across coastal dive hubs, seagrass meadows, mangrove shorelines, estuaries, and rocky reefs.

Initiative Blueprint, SMART Goals & Youth Outreach

Measurable Targets, Risk Mitigation & Seahorse Spotter

SMART Goals & 10-Year MPA Horizon

5-Year Target: Expand observation network coverage across 20 distinct coastal sectors.

10-Year Target: Submit crowdsourced data to local governments to legally establish 2 new permanent Marine Protected Areas (MPAs).

150 Volunteer Divers Trained (Yr 1)
300 Verified Sightings Logged (Yr 1)
5 Pilot Dive Sites Established

3 Primary Project Goals

  • Map undocumented seahorse populations and nursery zones along shallow coastal areas and estuaries.
  • Collect standardized environmental data (depth, temperature, substrate type) to assess habitat health.
  • Mobilize students, snorkelers, and beachgoers to contribute verifiable data to marine databases.

Challenges, Mitigation & Foreseen Successes

Identified Risks: Off-season drop-offs in participation during cold-water months and diver hesitation due to natural camouflage.

Mitigation Strategy: Host virtual off-season workshops and cooperative photo-verification badges before data visualization on public maps.

Foreseen Successes: Preventing Nursery Destruction (no-anchor zones against boat anchoring & coastal construction around seagrass beds) and Reducing Local Fishery Bycatch (partnering with regional fishers on low-impact zones).

Analyzing Data, Ecosystem Benefits & Coastal Field Sites

Part 2: Data Evaluation, Seagrass Blue Carbon & All 9 Coastal Field Photographs

Results Supporting Project Goals

Consistent multi-dive observations with verified sightings across healthy seagrass beds, paired with sustained volunteer participation mapping active breeding and nursery zones.

Results Modifying Project Goals

A steep year-over-year drop in sightings at historically active sites combined with high habitat degradation scores shifts goals from passive population mapping to active habitat restoration and emergency trawling protection lobbying.

Seagrass Blue Carbon

Seagrass meadows act as massive carbon sinks that absorb carbon up to 35x faster than tropical rainforests. Protecting seahorses preserves these vital carbon sinks.

Biodiversity & Nursery Hubs

Seagrass beds serve as critical nurseries for juvenile fish and invertebrates, supporting coastal fisheries and food security while filtering coastal waters and preventing erosion.

Broader Global Knowledge

Fills vital scientific data gaps regarding open-ocean population trends, supporting state, national, and international policy decisions for Marine Protected Areas (MPAs).
